Research Agenda

Computational Mediation

How do computational systems turn plural realities into selective representations?

What a system knows does not uniquely determine how it represents. I study the choices in between: the ways computational systems make some representations of people and communities more visible, accessible, or authoritative than others.

Beyond language: representation begins before generation

Two Ways of Seeing Recovery

Satellite imagery and ground inspection may not simply disagree in accuracy; they may operationalize different versions of “recovery.”

This project pushed me to think of representation as something shaped by measurement and operationalization, not only by language generation.

Open questions

  1. How can we map a system’s representational alternatives without assuming a single ground truth?
  2. What mechanisms systematically privilege one framing over another?
  3. How does representational structure change across prompts, languages, users, and contexts?
  4. How can interfaces make alternative representations visible without overwhelming users?
